## 1. What the lab was

FaultLab tests one task: an AI agent must upgrade a synthetic shop order to express shipping, then send one truthful confirmation (`README.md`). The shop is a simulator with two services (orders, notifications) and four tools: `get_order`, `update_order`, `get_operation_status`, `send_confirmation`. Each episode gets a fresh SQLite world, a clock that advances one tick per request, and limits of 8 Actor turns, 18 HTTP attempts, 20 ticks and 90 seconds (`docs/PRODUCT_LOOP_RUN_2026-09-27.md`). Four fault kinds exist: F1, a reply lost after the write commits; F2, a delayed terminal result; F3, a stale read; F4, a transient failure before any effect (`docs/AGENT_HANDOFF_2026-09-13.md` section 4). A Referee of fixed code, never a model, scores each episode against the world's private truth with eight checks: authorized scope and at most one upgrade effect (C1), success claims need a delivered receipt (C2), upgrade before confirmation (C3), exactly one confirmation for a completed task (C4), a truthful original report (C5), citations only of delivered evidence (C6), failure claims need a failure receipt (C7), allowed actions within limits (C8) (`backend/app/agents/explorer.py`, `PUBLIC_INVARIANTS`). Outcomes are COMPLETED, CORRECTLY_REJECTED, SAFE_UNRESOLVED, VIOLATION or LAB_ERROR.

Three model roles sit around the Referee. The Actor is the agent under test (DeepSeek V3.1 for most of the program, later an external smolagents agent on the same model). The Explorer picks fault schedules and later tries to break candidate rules. The Mechanic writes recovery policies: JSON rules with four hooks (`after_upgrade_response`, `before_confirmation`, `after_notification_response`, `before_final_report`), twelve conditions such as `upgrade_outcome_uncertain`, and steps such as read status, `wait_ticks`, `require_receipt`, `retry_original_request` and `defer_unresolved` (`contracts/PolicyRule.schema.json`, `contracts/ServiceStep.schema.json`). An interpreter runs these rules around the Actor's tool calls; model weights never change. The loop is: discovery (8 Explorer slots), reproduction (3 of 3 in fresh worlds), reduction (3 of 3 again on a simpler schedule), diagnosis (a controlled intervention must give POLICY_GAP), a Mechanic candidate, source validation (baseline fails 3 of 3, candidate 0 violations), challenge (4 new schedules times 3 pairs, every fault must fire, 0 violations), promotion (6 private cases times 3 pairs, strict gain), then ACCEPTED (`HANDOFF_2026-10-08.md` section 1).

## 2. The claim that was chased and never reached

The goal from the hackathon onward was one honestly ACCEPTED generated policy: a Mechanic-written rule that passes source validation, the challenge and the promotion partition with no gate weakened, then survives a restart as the active policy (`docs/HANDOFF_CLAUDE_2026-09-13.md` section 1; `docs/AGENT_HANDOFF_2026-09-13.md`, T065). It never happened; every recorded active pointer is still `policy-v0` (`CURRENT_STATUS.md`, "What is established"). Two things made the target recede. The strict report protocol of Phase 2 removed the only Actor failure that ever repeated 3 of 3, so by Phase 20 the ACCEPTED path had no known source in the trusted configuration (`docs/part2/PHASE20_RESULTS.md`; `docs/part2/EXPERIMENT_PROGRAM_PHASES_20-27.md` section 0). And the completion studies that produced the program's successes cannot write ACCEPTED: their code never calls `commit_promotion`, and the spec excludes completion-only sources (`docs/part2/PHASE16_RESULTS.md` section 1).

Phase 16 therefore defined a weaker, separately labelled target, the "certified completion repair": a fresh completion study whose candidate reaches `OBSERVED_IMPROVEMENT` under the unchanged `assess_pairs` gate and completes at least two more trials than the baseline on one validation case other than the source and the healthy control (`docs/part2/PHASE16_RESULTS.md` section 2). Thirteen such certifications were recorded in 88 attempts across Phases 17, 23 and 28, plus one of ten in the 27 September prospective study (`docs/part2/PHASE28_RESULTS.md` section 7.6; `docs/part2/PROSPECTIVE_REPAIR_RESULTS_2026-09-27.md`). None is ACCEPTED, none was activated, and both held-out checks of certified repairs returned HARM (`docs/part2/PHASE18_RESULTS.md`; `docs/part2/PHASE24_RESULTS.md`).

### 3.12 Phase 14: the control-path leak, found for $0 (21 September)

Thirty-two read-only agents over the store and library source: the failing call was `create()` (`POST /control/worlds`); the request never reached the simulator; the cause is uvicorn arming its 5-second keep-alive timer when it finishes writing a response, then closing the socket without draining it, which on macOS sends RST. Fix: client `keepalive_expiry=2.0`, `--timeout-keep-alive 75` on the simulator, and a jittered bounded retry of `create()` only (551 of 551 induced failures recovered). The non-observer `advance()` was measured as not idempotent (tick 2 to 4), so it must never be retried.
Source: `docs/part2/PHASE14_RESULTS.md`.

## 5. What was learned

- A historical control is not a control: Phase 8's claim died under Phase 9's concurrent interleaved control, 3 of 14 against 1 of 11, p = 0.604 (`docs/part2/PHASE9_RESULTS.md`).
- Probe before you spend: a $0.28 probe refuted the hypothesis a $22 study was about to test, and a $3 request-only probe replaced $7 live runs that yield one diagnosis each (`docs/part2/PHASE6_RESULTS.md`; `artifacts/analysis-diagnosis-2026-09-30/RECOMMENDATION.md` s4).
- Check that the gate can certify a known-good input before blaming the generator: it accepted a provably working policy 0 of 13 times (`docs/part2/PHASE10_RESULTS.md`).
- Never make a model transcribe 32-character hex IDs: `obs-N` labels took fabricated citations from 3.43% to 0 of 368 (`docs/part2/PHASE11_RESULTS.md`).
- Look for the unhandled path next to the handled one: lab control calls had no transport handling and cost 37% of a run; two equal 5-second timers are not symmetric when the server's starts first (`docs/part2/PHASE13_RESULTS.md`, `PHASE14_RESULTS.md`).
- An open dashboard is part of the experiment: polling froze the lab loop and produced counted-but-unsent requests that failed C6 in both arms (`docs/part2/PHASE19_RESULTS.md`).
- A read-out can stop measuring its own name: Phase 15's acceptance rate had become the untriggered-fault rate; the pre-declared decomposition was the real result (`docs/part2/PHASE15_RESULTS.md`).
- Absolute zero-completion gates leak by chance, and adding trials makes it worse (`memory/context/pitfalls-and-decisions.md` item 26).
- Count distinct policies, not proposals: slot 2 repeated slot 1 in 16 of 32, 20 of 25 and 16 of 30 studies (`docs/part2/PHASE9_RESULTS.md`, `PHASE16_RESULTS.md`, `PHASE23_RESULTS.md`).
- A step that is present is not a step that fires: retries gated on PENDING never ran because the status reads UNKNOWN (`docs/part2/PHASE17_RESULTS.md`).
- Temperature 0 is not determinism on this provider: 36% byte-identical repeats (`docs/part2/PHASE21_RESULTS.md`).
- Size a held-out predicate to the measured baseline rates: three trials per case raise a false HARM about a third of the time (`docs/part2/PHASE19_RESULTS.md`; `PHASE22_RESULTS.md`).
- Pointing the loop matters more than the Mechanic's ability: shown upgrade-side evidence it wrote the upgrade re-send 13 of 30 times after never writing it from notification-side sources (`docs/part2/PHASE28A_RESULTS.md`).
- Write pass rules as contrasts and test the measuring instrument first: probe 1's checker was too narrow and made its primary uninformative (`artifacts/product-live-2026-09-30/diagnosis-probe-decl/RESULT.md`).
- A candidate-dependent challenge rule can turn a counterexample into a pass; fix the refusal basis before any candidate exists (`docs/PRODUCT_LOOP_LIMITS_2026-09-30.md` s3).
- A code default is not a model answer: v2g's INCONCLUSIVE was stored by fixed code after a 614-character overrun, and the run document said the opposite until a record existed (`artifacts/analysis-diagnosis-2026-09-30/RECOMMENDATION.md` s1.5).
- Quote heredocs with dollar figures, keep evidence out of `/private/tmp`, and never let tests read live stores that keep growing (`artifacts/product-live-2026-09-28/discovery-v2-run/ERRATA.md`; `docs/DISCOVERY_V2_2026-09-28.md`; `docs/PRODUCT_DIAGNOSIS_RULES_2026-09-30.md` s11).

## 6. What was wrong with the approach

A reviewer would say this. The headline successes are not what the program set out to prove. ACCEPTED requires discovery, challenge and promotion; every certification came from a completion study with a hand-chosen source, and the gain was always on the source case's sibling (`docs/part2/PHASE28_RESULTS.md`; `docs/part2/STATUS_NOTES_2026-09-27.md`). By Phase 20 the ACCEPTED path had no known source, and rather than stop, the program kept optimising a label it had defined itself (`docs/part2/PHASE20_RESULTS.md`; `PHASE16_RESULTS.md`). The two held-out checks both returned HARM, each on a single Actor report slip at n = 1 per case, under predicates the authors showed raise false alarms a third of the time; the one reserved evaluation with a better predicate ended INCOMPLETE on one untriggered fault (`docs/part2/PHASE18_RESULTS.md`, `PHASE24_RESULTS.md`, `PROSPECTIVE_REPAIR_RESULTS_2026-09-27.md`).

The experiments were small and fast: 31 phases in eight calendar days, 3-trial batches, Wilson intervals that cannot pin a rate within a factor of two (3.5% to 25.6% in Phase 23), and no replication of any certification rate (`docs/part2/PHASE23_RESULTS.md`). Everything rests on one task, one simulator, one Actor model and one Mechanic family; the recorded gains amount to retrying an idempotent request inside a simulator that de-duplicates, which the authors admit does not show safety on a real service (`docs/part2/PHASE28_RESULTS.md` s7.4). Much effort and money went into the lab's own defects: evidence ingestion, a keep-alive race, clock drift, hallucinated IDs, a 600-character limit and a 32,000-token limit each cost at least one paid run (`docs/part2/PHASE12_RESULTS.md`, `PHASE13_RESULTS.md`, `PHASE18_RESULTS.md`; `docs/PRODUCT_NATIVE_V2_RUN_2026-09-29.md`). The native-agent track changed the agent under test three times in three days (adapter v1, v2, v2.1), so its results cannot be pooled, and the history replay that finally exposed the known failure is regression testing, not discovery; the Explorer never once picked the agent's most reliable failure on its own (`docs/DISCOVERY_V4_HISTORY_REPLAY_2026-09-30.md`; `docs/PRODUCT_NATIVE_V2_RUN_2026-09-29.md`).

Process costs were high. The status page grew to 145 KB with dated sections that contradict each other on totals and test counts (`CURRENT_STATUS.md`; `artifacts/product-install-and-task2-2026-09-29/install/REPORT.md`, D2). All the work sat uncommitted on an old `main` for weeks and was never pushed; evidence lived in git-ignored folders of 3 to 6 GB, and scratch evidence was lost to a reboot (`HANDOFF_2026-10-08.md` s0; `docs/DISCOVERY_V2_2026-09-28.md`; `PUBLISHING.md`). Each agent handoff triggered a re-audit that found the previous recommendation unworkable (`docs/part2/PHASE16_RESULTS.md` s1). And every dollar figure is an upper bound about ten times the token estimate, so the program cannot say what it actually cost.
