The historical notes below explain earlier experiments and are not a description of every current code path.

Phase 3 adds duplicate schedule handling and a separate completion study, not a relaxed promotion gate. Its two generated policies completed zero of three source tasks each. Both misunderstood waiting versus submitting a previously rejected write. The one alternate-Mechanic experiment stopped on an untriggered control fault before making any Mechanic call; it does not evaluate V4 repair ability. All 34 episode traces are now verified, but neither positive completion validation nor ordinary policy acceptance was reached. The Actor also sometimes polls a notification it never attempted. No provider or engine error explains that behavior; Phase 4 reproduced it live and showed that telling the Actor about its own attempts does not fix it.

Phase 6 asked why. A non-scoring comprehension probe put the question to both models directly and **refuted the leading explanation**: six of eight answers said replaying an already-dispatched write is permitted and quoted the contract sentence that settles it, and none said forbidden. Clarifying the brief's "first writes" wording then changed **zero** proposals — across Phases 5 and 6 the count is 0 of 6. Phase 6 also measured, for the first time, that the declared source gap is **4.4% leaky**: the baseline completes the source case in 2 of 45 valid triggered episodes, so a three-trial zero-completion gate stops by chance roughly 13% of the time, which is exactly how study 6B ended. That is a power limitation of the design, recorded for a prospective fix and explicitly not repaired by loosening any threshold.

Phase 7 located the loss with two more non-scoring probes and killed both remaining explanations. Asked what happened to the notification operation, all four responses quoted the delivered `TRANSIENT_UNAVAILABLE` but **none could state the operation was never accepted**, and one answered that "the supplied development evidence contains no failing episodes" because every C1-C8 check passed. Handed a complete, correct diagnosis outright, **0 of 4** responses emitted the repair step. The declared Phase 7 intervention was dropped rather than run, because the probe had invalidated its premise.

Phase 8 acted on what the probes found and bound the adjudicated per-episode outcome into the evidence the Mechanic reads — a presentation change that disclosed nothing new, since the same verdict was already supplied in grouped form. **The predeclared read-out moved for the first time: 2 of 4 proposals contain the repair step**, and the V4 candidate completed the source case live in 2 of 3 trials, with the policy's replay landing a SUCCEEDED receipt at tick 8. It still did not qualify: `assess_pairs` requires 3 of 3 and the third episode failed C6, as did an independent **baseline** episode in the same batch. Read that result at its true size — both proposals were the same byte-identical policy, so one model produced **one distinct policy**, and the V3.1 control did not change by a single byte from its Phase 5 output. **No generated policy has been accepted.** The binding constraint has moved from the Mechanic to Actor evidence-citation (C6) failures under an absolute completion gate.

Phase 9 replicated Phase 8 properly and **it did not hold**. A paired, interleaved run with a *concurrent* control — 32 studies, 16 attempts per arm, both arms on identical code and one backend, leading arm swapped each round — produced **3 of 14 in the treatment against 1 of 11 in the control, Fisher's exact two-sided p = 0.604**. The control arm produced the repair step too. Phase 8's causal claim is therefore **withdrawn**: a 21% event was observed once and over-read because its only control was three historical studies. The design had ~1.00 power against a large effect, so a large effect is excluded; an effect of 30–40% is not, and the result is reported as "no effect large enough for this design to see". The protocol was adversarially reviewed by six independent lenses before any paid call — 47 findings, 30 confirmed — which caught, among other things, a read-out bug that would have silently resampled every Mechanic refusal and biased the headline.

Phase 9's more important result is elsewhere. **A generated policy qualified on the source case for the first time**: study T15's candidate passed `assess_pairs` with 3 of 3 candidate completions and 0 baseline completions, the only such candidate in the project's history. On the frozen 42-episode validation batch it beat the baseline on four cases and regressed on none, then was correctly rejected for a single violation on `notification-18` — where **the Actor hallucinated an evidence ID**, citing `evidence-a20c6f2a5a8547f1b445a025extremely-9372`, which exists nowhere in the store, alongside 12 real ones. The baseline failed the same case independently on C5/C6. **No policy was accepted**, but the binding constraint has moved and is now measured: candidate episodes violate C5/C6 at 16–28% against ~3% for baseline episodes, and the repair itself works whenever it is proposed — all four step-containing candidates completed the source case at least 2 of 3.

**Phase 10 changed what this project has been measuring.** Phase 9's qualifying policy was replayed through the frozen validation batch 26 times, through the unchanged gate code. **0 of 13 scored replays were accepted** (Wilson 95% [0.000, 0.228]). A repair that provably completes the task is certified essentially never.

The cause is not the policy. Candidate-arm episodes violate a check 26 times in 414 (**6.3%**); baseline episodes 21 times in 414 (**5.1%**) — an excess of about 1.2 points. Phase 9's 16–28% candidate figure was measured on the source case alone and did not generalise. **62% of all violating episodes (29 of 47) cite an evidence ID that does not exist.** The mechanism is transcription, not reasoning: the Actor copies 32-character opaque hex identifiers and corrupts one about **3.4%** of the time, measured on 1,284 reports — a rate predicted to within 0.2 points by a pre-registered analysis of 466 reports before the run. The literal token **"extremely" has been spliced into a hex identifier four separate times** across Phases 8–10; other identifiers lose characters. The failure concentrates where there is most to copy: `notification-18` cites 13–14 of 15 observations and is the worst case in **both** arms.

The honest restatement is that FaultLab has generated a recovery policy that completes the task, and its acceptance gate cannot currently certify one, because the gate's unit of evidence is an Actor-transcribed identifier corrupted about 6% of episodes. **The gate is not loosened** — it is doing what it was built to do; what must change is the reliability of the evidence it judges. A second limitation of equal cost: one transient provider timeout makes an episode `LAB_ERROR`, the evidence gateway then refuses it, and the entire 42-episode batch aborts — 13 of 26 Phase 10 attempts died that way, and only 8 of 26 produced a clean quality verdict.

**Phase 11 removed the transcription, and the failure went with it.** A new declared protocol shows the Actor `obs-1 … obs-n` instead of 32-character identifiers; cited labels are decoded by exact lookup, with unmatched tokens passed through verbatim so they still fail C6. `checks.py` was never opened and `scorer_hash` is unchanged. Across **370 episodes there were zero fabricated identifiers** (against a 3.43% baseline), the ≥12-citation regime went from 30% corrupted to **0 of 14**, and the **C1–C8 violation rate fell from 6% to 0%**. Replays rejected on policy quality went from **8 of 13 to 0 of 7 (p = 0.015)**.

Two honest qualifications. The **declared primary read-out — how many replays the gate accepted — is 2 of 6, p = 0.088, which does not reach significance**; the pre-declared pooled figure including the Stage A validity check is 3 of 7, p = 0.031, and both are reported rather than either alone. And the binding constraint has moved again: **every remaining non-pass is batch fragility**, not policy quality — one transient provider error aborts a whole 42-episode batch through the evidence gateway, and at a 97.4% fault-trigger rate roughly 46% of batches contain at least one untriggered fault. The Phase 4 regression guard passes: no case lost its target outcome, none gained a violation, fault triggering and Actor behaviour are unchanged. One pre-registered concern came out backwards and is recorded as such — judges predicted cheap labels would cause blanket citing; citation breadth instead *fell* from 0.887 to 0.735, meaning the Actor became more selective, which is the harder direction for C6 to satisfy.

**Phase 15 reached the read-out the project had been short of since Phase 11.** Twenty scored replays, nine accepted, against a control of zero in thirteen — a two-sided exact p of 0.005, with a significance threshold fixed in advance and met. It also replicates Phase 13 exactly. The control-path fix held completely: **not one of the twenty-eight attempts died the way eleven of thirty did last time**, a difference at p = 0.0003, and the yield objective set two phases earlier was finally met.

**But the headline does not mean what its name suggests, and the protocol said so before the run.** Once citation corruption was removed, the gate effectively stopped rejecting this policy on quality — so whether a replay passes now turns mostly on whether every fault in the batch actually fired, a nuisance rate that had already moved by a factor of two between the phases being compared. Quoting the headline without that rate beside it would let a drift in fault triggering read as a change in the gate. The figure that isolates the gate was therefore declared in advance, and it is the cleanest result this project has produced: **among batches where every fault triggered, nine of ten were accepted; among batches where one did not, none were.** When the measurement is clean, the gate certifies the working repair nine times in ten.

**Two honest qualifications.** Quality rejection is near-eliminated but **no longer zero** — one replay in twenty was rejected on a clean batch for a genuine regression within a validation case, the first such rejection since Phase 10, so "zero quality rejections" is no longer the right phrase. And nothing has been promoted: replay mode runs validation only and cannot accept a policy.

**One loss channel remains, and it is now the only one that is a defect rather than a design choice.** Evidence verification killed eight of twenty-eight attempts. The untriggered-fault channel costs half the scored replays and is deliberately left fatal, because tolerating an untriggered fault would weaken the gate.

**The pre-run review earned its cost again.** Four independent lenses attacked the protocol before any money moved and all four said it needed changes — including a false integrity claim of mine about which source hashes had moved, and two latent defects in the runner that could silently delete a verdict the gate had already reached, both biased toward discarding the replays that were scorable.

**Phase 14 identified the failing control call for $0, and could not explain it.** Phase 13's largest loss channel was an unhandled `httpx.ReadError` that killed 11 of 30 attempts. It is `create()` — `POST /control/worlds` — and the identification is structural rather than statistical: `execute_slots` wraps `runner.run` in no try block, `runner.run` has no `finally` and two blanket `except Exception` handlers, and `create()` is the only HTTP call in the window before the episode is written. All 14 such studies in the store leave exactly one slot `INTERRUPTED` with no episode record; all 56 evidence-channel studies leave zero. The failing request never reached the simulator: no world file carries any of the 14 episode identifiers, no orphan world exists with a Phase 13 timestamp, and an instrumented server counted 884 requests for 884 client successes.

**A second free round found the cause, in the web server's own source.** uvicorn starts its keep-alive countdown when it finishes *writing* a response, while the HTTP client starts its equivalent timer when it finishes *reading* one — so although both default to five seconds, the server's deadline always falls first, leaving a window in which the client legitimately reuses a connection the server is about to drop. uvicorn's timeout handler then closes the socket without first reading what is in it, and on macOS closing a socket that holds unread bytes sends a reset rather than a polite shutdown. The request is discarded before the application ever runs — which is exactly why no world was ever created — and the client sees the reset as a read error. Every observation follows from this: the threshold sits at precisely five seconds, the error is always a reset and never a clean disconnect, and the failure rate *decays* over the following half-second rather than holding steady, because the window is the event loop's scheduling latency.

**An earlier framing of mine was a false premise.** I had insisted any explanation must also account for zero failures beyond six seconds. There is no such edge: the decay model predicts well under one failure there, and the exposures beyond six seconds are not comparable anyway, since gap length is driven by how much telemetry the preceding episode produced. The apparent upper edge dissolves under stratification while the lower edge survives every test.

**So the real repair is connection lifetime, not retrying.** The client now retires an idle connection after two seconds against the server's five, so the client is always the side that closes and the server never drops a socket with a request in it — verified deterministically rather than by chasing a two-millisecond race: at 4.9 seconds idle the old client reused the connection, which is the dangerous state, and the new one opens a fresh one. The simulator is also launched with a much longer keep-alive as defence in depth. Neither touches a handler, a response, a fixture, a fault schedule or anything the referee reads; only TCP connection lifetime changes. The bounded retry remains as a backstop, and review found two real defects in its first version — it had no jitter, so a fixed ladder could resonate with a periodic cause, and its backoff table was indexed without a clamp.

**The retry is safe for that one call and deliberately for no other.** Nothing anywhere resolves a world from an episode identifier, and no untriggered-fault statistic scans the worlds directory, so a lost creation leaves a world the lab cannot even name and the fatal-untriggered-fault rule is untouched. Measured against the real simulator, a repeated non-observer `advance` takes the clock from 2 to 4 — it double-applies, and a double-applied advance can change whether a scheduled fault triggered, hence whether a trial is valid, hence whether a pair can qualify. That is a silent change to what the gate measures, so an anti-regression test asserts the other four operations are called exactly once. Two earlier statements are corrected here: the *observer* advance is in fact idempotent, latched atomically with its tick; and the protected teardown calls did **not** fail zero times — three episodes lost their teardown and each one killed a whole study through the evidence gateway, a quieter route that this fix deliberately leaves open for a separate, measured change.

**Phase 12 could not measure its own change, and Phase 13 did.** Phase 12 shipped a bounded, blind retry of the Actor's model call on the two codes that mean no generation arrived, then spent 28 attempts and $8.15 measuring nothing because W&B Weave trace ingestion had degraded from a 0.9% to a 31% per-episode failure rate. That was not a code regression — 0 lab errors in 91 episodes — and it is why `scripts/experiments/check_ingestion_health.py` now runs before any paid batch.

**Phase 13 re-ran the measurement and the gate accepted the policy: 5 of 13 scored replays returned `OBSERVED_IMPROVEMENT`** (Wilson 95% [0.177, 0.645]) against Phase 10's 0 of 13, Fisher two-sided **p = 0.039**, with **zero quality rejections** — every non-pass was `INCOMPLETE`, none `NO_CHANGE`. Pooled with Phase 11 that is 0 quality rejections in 20 scored replays against Phase 10's 8 of 13. Phase 11, not Phase 13, was the first phase to get a replay accepted (2 of 6); what Phase 13 adds is roughly double the scored n and a single declared figure, where Phase 11's significance depended on which of two pre-declared figures you quoted.

Three qualifications, all of which belong with the number. **The declared target was 20 scored replays and the run reached 13** at its 30-attempt cap; the shortfall is reported rather than the read-out re-declared, and at n=13 the design cannot separate 0.38 from 0.25 or 0.60. **Phase 12's actual objective — raising yield — is unmet**: 13 of 30 attempts scored, 0.433 against an all-prior 0.513 and a predicted ~0.6, Fisher p = 0.63. And nothing has been promoted: replay mode runs validation only and cannot accept a policy.

**The Phase 12 retry does work, on a historical control.** Actor transport failures: Phase 10 had 10 and none of those episodes survived; Phase 13 had 5, retried 4, and all 4 survived (Fisher p = 0.0037, disjoint Wilson intervals). This is a historical rather than concurrent comparison and is always to be quoted that way. The one failure that was *not* retried exposed a real defect: the retry guard read a call counter that `meter.model_call()` had already incremented, so on the last of eight allowed turns it compared `8 < 8` and never fired — on precisely the turn that carries the report. The same design also charged a turn for a call that delivered no generation, leaving an episode 7 reasoning turns instead of 8. Both are fixed by admitting the retry separately: it charges dollars and model calls but no actor turn, and the `actor_calls` bound is unchanged. That fix is built, tested and **unmeasured**.

**The binding constraint has moved to a path nobody had examined.** Of Phase 13's 17 unscored attempts, **11 (37% of all attempts) died of an unhandled `httpx.ReadError`** — more than the evidence gateway (5) and everything else combined. `WorldClient.control()`, the route for world creation, tick advance, stop, snapshot and fault listing, had no transport handling at all, while the business-tool path two methods below it has always converted transport errors into a tool result. One dropped read on a localhost control call aborted a whole 42-episode batch. Phase 13 **instrumented rather than fixed** it: the failing operation is now named, but nothing is retried, because `advance` and `create` are not idempotent and replaying an `advance` whose response was lost could double-advance the world. Which of the five operations fails is still unknown. The evidence channel itself was healthy in Phase 13 (0.6% per-episode ingestion failures, 5 of 848), and the untriggered-fault channel remains a deliberate cost rather than a defect — 8 of the 13 scored replays ended `INCOMPLETE` that way, and tolerating an untriggered fault would weaken the gate.

Phase 5 finally ran the experiment Phase 3 B2 died before reaching: asking a Mechanic for a completion-recovery policy with the DeepSeek V4-Pro-0813 model, and a matched V3.1 control. Two proposals each. **None of the four contained `retry_original_request` for notifications — the one step that completes the task.** Both models' second proposals were byte-identical to their first while their explanations claimed to be materially different; V3.1's even described "retrying the original send_confirmation request" in prose while emitting a policy without it. Both studies ended NO_CHANGE. The repair is demonstrably available: the same runtime path used by the handwritten reference actor completes 9 of 9 source trials. Why the models avoid it is not established — no reasoning trace is retained — and three separate untested hypotheses are recorded in the Phase 5 results.

Phase 4 measured the Actor's per-turn context directly. Two declared 60-episode comparisons, 120 of 120 traces verified, both ended **REGRESSION**. Giving the Actor a truthful summary of its own attempted writes changed what it did — it resent a rejected confirmation and stopped re-reading a status it had never started, completing `notification-3` once in three against zero in three for the control — and it also made the Actor write `NOT_STARTED` where the report contract requires `NOT_REQUIRED`, turning a clean `CORRECTLY_REJECTED` into a C5 violation in five of six changed-arm trials. Nothing was adopted; `faultlab-actor-context/v1` remains the default. The control arm is not clean either: it invented a `PENDING` notification twice and, once, never sent the confirmation at all, leaving its scheduled fault untriggered. A context change that is more accurate is not automatically better, and completion counts alone would have hidden the regression.

The follow-up that withheld the summary on report-required turns did not work, because this Actor volunteers its report before the reserved final turn in most episodes (27 of 30 in the changed arm). `final_report_required` is a floor the engine enforces, not a signal that the current turn carries the report.

Completion studies freeze their plan, never replace invalid or untriggered trials, and cannot resume after interruption. The context study deliberately differs: it retains an invalid or untriggered trial, names its case as incomplete, excludes that case from the decision and finishes the remaining predeclared slots. That is right for a measurement batch and would be wrong for a promotion gate. A separate evidence synchronization can verify saved traces but cannot resume the study or rewrite its decision. Development validation is not the protected final audit. The shared Phase 2–6 $1,000 ceiling has $43.69230 conservatively charged across 2,049 requests; actual billed dollars remain unavailable.

## Historical findings before Phase 1

FaultLab implements one scope: upgrade an authorized order to express shipping and record one truthful simulated confirmation. Notifications never send real email. The four ordinary fault primitives preserve the frozen service contract. C1–C8 score actual private effects and the original actor report against delivered public evidence.

Real model execution, campaign trace readback and a 36-trial Llama baseline evaluation were verified; those historical learning outcomes are in `docs/learning-results.md`. The V3.1 campaign completed 119 episodes and 829 model calls with no provider errors and all 119 episode traces verified, but no diagnosis qualified for repair. These results do not establish learned improvement or independent transfer. Aria was deferred at that checkpoint and subsequently tested as described below. All malformed, flaky, untriggered, rejected, interrupted and no-gain attempts remain evidence.
