# Findings and mistakes not to repeat

1. **Successful business effects are not enough.** Original reports must be truthful, use delivered matching evidence and arrive inside the unchanged limits. Phase 2 strict schema fixed many malformed reports, not semantic errors. Phase 3 still found an actual citation typo.
2. **UNKNOWN is not PENDING.** It may describe an operation never submitted or not publicly known. A timeout may hide a committed write. Preserve uncertainty and identity rather than inventing acceptance or failure.
3. **Reading status does not create an operation.** B's Mechanic proposed waiting/polling twice despite the explicit runtime contract and zero-completion feedback. Those were bad proposals; the engine executed them correctly. Do not repair the policy by hand and call it model-generated.
4. **Hook timing matters.** `after_notification_response` runs after an Actor notification attempt and exhausted adapter retries. Policy actions do not recursively invoke hooks. `before_final_report` runs only after a valid report proposal; it cannot rescue a missing report. A receipt requirement does not make a missing operation succeed and does not block an honest unresolved report.
5. **Source reproducibility matters.** An isolated C6 mistake that reproduces 0/3 cannot qualify as a reliable invariant-repair source. A completion gap is different from a C1–C8 violation. Do not change the original gate to count safe unresolved as unsafe.
6. **Identical policy or schedule is not a new experiment.** Policy-content deduplication and normalized fault-schedule deduplication preserve attempts without fresh qualification chances. Seeds are metadata, not a reason to rerun a duplicate schedule. Live Phase 3 had eight unique selections, so actual duplicate rejection was checked offline, not falsely claimed as a live event.
7. **A planned fault may never trigger.** A's two unreachable schedules and B2's skipped notification are retained. B2 had no infrastructure/provider error and no V4 Mechanic call; it cannot support any conclusion about V4 repair quality. Generic saved `StoreConflict` stop metadata is not a root-cause diagnosis; inspect the actual trial.
8. **Do not retry closed studies for a nicer outcome.** B stopped after its two declared candidates. B2 was one prospective model-change experiment and is now closed. Start a newly declared phase if continuing, with fresh identity and all old results retained.
9. **Evidence synchronization is not a model rerun.** Weave readback can complete after an experiment stops. B2's original study evidence map remains four/five while five/five global episode traces are now verified. Never rewrite the decision to match later evidence.
10. **No private or protected evidence in optimization.** Referee truth, actual simulator state and protected final/transfer cases are verifier-only. Actor progress must derive only from its attempted requests and delivered public observations. Completion validation is fixed before execution and never sent back to the Mechanic.
11. **Limits are part of the experiment.** Eight Actor turns include the report turn. Unlimited credits do not authorize silently adding turns, provider retries or relaxed budgets inside a frozen comparison. Change configurations explicitly for future experiments.
12. **Runtime identity includes algorithms.** Restart the reviewed source with explicit settings before paid work. Do not edit frozen backend files mid-run; old processes can otherwise execute different code than the checkout. A new context schema changes the experiment identity even if the model is unchanged.
13. **Trace classification and historical mistakes are retained.** Older reports sometimes classified agent report failures as infrastructure faults; new traces were fixed. Some older native/regression evidence had missing spans. Consult phase reports rather than assuming all historical data uses current semantics.
14. **Respect the dirty checkout.** Phase 1–3 source, tests, docs and generated contracts are not all committed. Never use destructive reset/checkout or stash-away without preserving them. Current historical handoffs' branch, budget and status claims are stale.
15. **Frontend defects found manually were fixed.** Identical sibling React keys caused repeated study panels while polling. Unique keys now separate search and completion. Queued wording no longer says no calls started during execution; source-assessment reasons are displayed; incomplete studies explain untriggered faults. Keep these behaviors when editing.
16. **Outcome labels are not interchangeable.** NO_CHANGE is a valid scientific result; INCOMPLETE records a stop; COMPLETED can describe experiment lifecycle rather than task outcome; OBSERVED_IMPROVEMENT in the separate study is still not ACCEPTED. No such positive completion result exists yet.

17. **More accurate input is not automatically better.** Phase 4 gave the Actor a truthful summary of its own attempted writes. It retried a rejected write more often and wasted fewer status reads, and it also started reporting `NOT_STARTED` where the contract requires `NOT_REQUIRED`, turning a clean `CORRECTLY_REJECTED` into a C5 violation 5 times in 6 live trials. Measure a context change against a case where the correct report word is not the literal description of events; `terminal-upgrade-rejection` is the known-sensitive case.
18. **`final_report_required` is a floor, not the report turn.** The Actor volunteers its report before the reserved turn in most episodes (22/30, 29/30, 19/30, 27/30 across the four Phase 4 arms). Any rule that wants to treat reporting differently needs another signal. The Phase 4 follow-up failed for exactly this reason; do not repeat it.
19. **Retain invalid trials in a measurement batch.** Phase 4 kept two untriggered trials and finished both 60-episode batches. The completion study's abort-on-invalid rule would have destroyed each run at trial 6, which is what happened to Phase 3 B2. Aborting is right for a promotion gate and wrong for a comparison.
20. **A safety check must not be conditional on comparability.** The first context study computed case completeness across BOTH arms and then counted violations only over complete cases, so one invalid trial in the *control* arm deleted the changed arm's C1-C8 violations from the decision. Flipping one real control trial to untriggered turns that study's own rule from REGRESSION into OBSERVED_IMPROVEMENT. Worse, a fault counts as triggered only when the Actor calls the faulted tool, so an arm that degrades to never writing invalidates its own trial and buys its case an exemption. Count safety over every case; restrict only the comparison.
21. **A claim about an operation cannot come from the last attempt alone.** `write_rejected_before_acceptance` looked only at the most recent write response. With a reply lost after a commit and a documented refusal on the retry, it said "rejected" while the world held a receipt and a committed effect. Quantify over every delivered attempt, and never let a timeout anywhere in the history license a rejection claim.
22. **Regenerating contracts invalidates every artifact frozen against the old ones.** Adding the `P1` arm and the `context_study` purpose changed `RegressionBundle.schema.json` and the global `contract_hash`, so all 6 stored regression bundles, all 3 exported ones and 25 of 26 stored configurations stopped validating. That is the integrity check working, not a bug. Do not add a legacy hash escape hatch; re-export under the current contracts if the capability is needed, as a separately declared action.
23. **Review your own new code adversarially before building on it.** Six independent reviewers plus a refutation pass over the Phase 4 work produced 18 surviving findings, including three real code defects and four wrong numbers in the write-up, none of which the 544-test suite caught. It cost no provider budget. Do it before the next paid run, not after.
24. **Ask before you intervene.** A $0.28 non-scoring comprehension probe killed the hypothesis a $22 study was about to test: the models do not believe a replay is forbidden, they say PERMITTED and quote the contract. Probe first when the hypothesis is about what a model knows or believes.
25. **Write the selection rule so it cannot fire degenerately.** Phase 6's rule said "forbidden OR omits the op from Q1" in the with-brief condition "while" the without-brief condition permits it. The omission appeared in BOTH conditions, so the rule fired without the contrast it was written to detect. State contrasts as contrasts.
26. **The source gap is 95.6%, not 100%.** The baseline Actor completes the declared source case in 2 of 45 valid triggered episodes. The controls gate wants zero completions in three trials, so it stops by chance ~13% of the time, and `assess_pairs` fails a perfect candidate at the same rate. **Corrected in Phase 8:** declaring more *trials* makes it worse, not better — against an absolute zero-completion gate every extra trial is another chance to leak. The only honest prospective mitigation is more independent *runs*, capped and declared in advance and permitted only after a stop that produced no proposal. Never loosen the threshold after a stop.

27. **A declared intervention can be invalidated by the probe that was supposed to justify it.** Phase 7's selection rule fired cleanly and selected H2 — de-interning the error codes — but the probe had just found the error code four times out of four, so H2 addressed a retrieval problem that did not exist. The honest move is to say so, drop the arm, and declare a new phase in the open. Recording "the previously declared arm was not run, and why" is what keeps that from being selection on the data.

28. **Presentation is not disclosure, but you must prove it.** Phase 8 added the adjudicated `task_outcome` to each episode row and moved the read-out from 0 of 6 to 2 of 4. That is defensible only because the same verdict was already in the Mechanic's `observed_outcomes`, the semantics were already in `completion.md`, and both facts were checked against the saved Phase 5 context *before* the change was written. Check the premise against saved evidence first; do not assert it.

29. **Count distinct policies, not proposals.** Phases 5, 6 and 8 each produced a second proposal that was byte-identical to the first while its explanation claimed material novelty. A read-out defined over proposals therefore double-counts. Report both numbers: Phase 8's "2 of 2" is one distinct policy.

30. **A working repair can still be rejected by the Actor, not the policy.** Phase 8's candidate executed correctly and completed 2 of 3, failing the third on C6 — the Actor cited evidence that did not support its own `COMPLETED` claim. An independent *baseline* episode in the same batch failed C5 and C6 too. When the gate is an absolute `L completed == 3`, Actor reporting variance, not Mechanic quality, decides the outcome.

31. **A historical control is not a control.** Phase 8 compared a single treatment study against Phases 5/6, run days earlier on different code, and read a causal effect into it. Phase 9's concurrent, interleaved control gave T 3 of 14 vs C 1 of 11 (p = 0.604) and withdrew the claim. If the comparison arm did not run today, alongside, the claim is about time as much as treatment.

32. **Review the protocol AND the runner adversarially before spending, not after.** Six lenses raised 47 findings on the Phase 9 design; 30 survived refutation. The worst would have scored every Mechanic refusal as "no draw" and resampled that study — deleting only zeros, from whichever arm refuses more, and biasing the headline invisibly. Convergence across independent lenses is the signal: six found that one.

33. **State the power the design had, including what it could not have seen.** Phase 9 excludes a large effect (power ~1.00) but not a 30–40% one (power ~0.39). "Not significant" is not "no effect"; write the distinction down.

34. **When a repair finally qualifies, check whether the thing that stopped it is the policy at all.** Phase 9's T15 candidate passed the source gate and lost validation to a single episode in which the *Actor* invented an evidence ID (`…a025extremely-9372`) among 12 real ones. The baseline failed the same case independently. Candidate episodes violate C5/C6 at 16-28% against ~3% for baselines — the binding constraint is Actor reporting reliability, not Mechanic quality.

35. **Check whether your gate can certify a known-good input before concluding anything about the generator.** For ten phases "no accepted policy" was read as evidence about the Mechanic. Phase 10 replayed a policy that provably completes the task through the unchanged gate 26 times and it was accepted **0 of 13**. The gate was never the question being answered.

36. **When a check fails, ask what its unit of evidence actually is.** C6 requires exact evidence IDs, and the Actor transcribes 32-character opaque hex strings from memory. It corrupts one about 3.4% of the time - the token `extremely` has been spliced into a hex ID four separate times, and other IDs simply lose characters. That is a decoding artifact, not a reasoning failure, and it made candidate and baseline arms violate at nearly the same rate (6.3% vs 5.1%).

37. **Look for the mechanism in retained data before spending.** Phase 10's entire causal story was found for $0 by classifying citations in 466 saved reports, and the 828-episode run then replicated the rate to within 0.2 points. Declare such a prior in the protocol so the result cannot be read as fitted to it.

38. **One lab error should not destroy a batch.** A transient provider timeout makes an episode `LAB_ERROR`; the evidence gateway refuses it and the whole 42-episode study aborts. 13 of 26 Phase 10 attempts died this way. The Phase 6 retain-and-continue fix covers untriggered trials but not lab errors.

39. **Stop at the declared cap and report the shortfall.** Phase 10 targeted 20 scored replays and reached its 26-attempt cap at 13. The cap was not raised to reach the number; 0 of 13 was reported with its interval.

40. **Check that your measurement vehicle can see the effect before you pay for it.** Phase 11's obvious A/B was a context study. It cannot detect citation corruption at all: all 120 Phase 4 context-study episodes had zero, because corruption needs >=12 citations and only a *running policy* produces that many observations. It would have cost $8 to compare 0% with 0%, and that null was a live risk of being read as "the fix does not matter".

41. **Look hard for regressions in your own change, especially when it touches the Actor.** Phase 4 is the precedent. Phase 11 ran a per-case target-outcome guard, a fault-trigger comparison (97.5% vs 97.4%) and an action-profile comparison (5.42 vs 5.14 calls) before claiming success. All passed - but running them is what makes the result reportable.

42. **Record predictions that come out backwards.** Three design judges predicted cheap citation labels would make blanket citing free and erode C6. Citation breadth *fell* instead, 0.887 -> 0.735: the Actor became more selective, which is the HARDER direction because C6's sub-clauses require the supporting receipt to be cited.

43. **Quote the declared read-out even when a pre-declared alternative looks better.** Phase 11's primary is 2 of 6 (p = 0.088, not significant); the pre-declared pooled figure is 3 of 7 (p = 0.031). Both are reported, never one alone. A result that depends on which pre-declared figure you quote is not conclusive.

44. **Design by panel when the constraints are tight.** Five independent proposals, three judging lenses each, surfaced the blanket-citation objection and killed a weaker identifier-shape design that the store's own data refuted - all before a line was written.

45. **Check the external dependency's health before starting a long paid run.** Phase 12 spent 28 attempts and $8.15 producing nothing because W&B Weave ingestion had degraded from a 0.9% to a 31% per-episode failure rate; a 42-episode batch needs every episode verified, so no batch could complete. `scripts/experiments/check_ingestion_health.py` is read-only and free; run it first.

46. **Give long unattended runs a circuit breaker.** 28 identical failures should have stopped after 4. The runner now aborts when consecutive attempts yield no assessment.

47. **Do not blame your own change before checking.** On seeing 28 failures the first call was "a regression I introduced". It was not: 0 LAB_ERROR episodes in 91, 0 new provider failures, and the new retry never fired. Read the evidence before attributing.

48. **A guard that gives false confidence is worse than no guard.** The first version of the ingestion health check reported HEALTHY during a 23% failure rate, because its SQL had no ORDER BY and the code then sliced the unordered result. Recorded rather than quietly corrected.

49. **Retrying a transport failure is not resampling; retrying a delivered response is.** `CONNECTION_ERROR` and `REQUEST_TIMEOUT` mean no generation arrived, so nothing was observed and nothing can be selected on - the retry is blind. `EMPTY_FINAL_RESPONSE` is a response that did arrive, so retrying it re-rolls a generation, and it is deliberately excluded.

50. **Know which table your evidence lives in before concluding a feature never fired.** FaultLab stores episode events in a dedicated `events` table (`episode_id, seq, payload`), *not* in `records` with `kind='events'`, and the event's `type` is inside the JSON payload. Querying `records` returned 0 `actor_transport_retry` events, which I read as "the retry never fired" and announced as a bug in my own change. It had fired 4 times and all 4 episodes survived. A zero from a query you have not validated is not a measurement.

51. **A counter incremented before a guard reads it makes the guard unreachable at the boundary.** The Phase 12 retry guard was `meter.usage.actor_calls < meter.caps.actor_calls`, but `meter.model_call()` runs before the `try` and increments that counter. On the last allowed turn the guard therefore compared `8 < 8` and suppressed the retry - on precisely the turn that carries the report and whose loss is most expensive. One of Phase 13's five transport failures hit there and killed a 42-episode batch. When a guard reads state that an earlier line in the same block mutates, check the boundary case explicitly.

52. **A resource cap should count what it means to bound.** The same design silently cost an episode a whole reasoning turn: a single transport retry left the Actor with 7 turns instead of 8, because a call that delivered *no generation* was charged as a turn. `actor_calls` is meant to bound generations (contract bound `le=8`). The fix - `EpisodeMeter.transport_retry()`, which charges dollars and `model_calls` but no turn - restores that meaning without raising the bound. The old test asserted `actor_calls == len(requests) + 1` and had been pinning the bug in plain sight.

53. **A retry must never replace the failure that caused it with a new one.** `transport_retry()` returns False rather than raising when it cannot be admitted, so a budget or stop condition during a retry surfaces the original transport error. Otherwise a recovery mechanism becomes a new failure mode and the diagnosis points at the wrong thing.

54. **Look for the unhandled path next to the handled one.** `WorldClient.call()` has always converted `httpx.TimeoutException` and `httpx.TransportError` into a `ToolResult`. `WorldClient.control()`, two methods above it and the route for `create`, `advance`, `stop`, `snapshot` and `faults`, had **no transport handling at all**. One dropped localhost read aborted a whole 42-episode batch, and this was the single largest failure channel in Phase 13 - 11 of 30 attempts, 37%, more than the Weave and Actor channels combined. Three phases of batch-fragility work had never looked there.

55. **Instrument before retrying something that is not idempotent.** The obvious reflex on item 54 is to retry control calls. `advance()` and `create()` are not idempotent - replaying an `advance` whose response was lost could double-advance the world and corrupt the episode. Phase 13 therefore only added `WorldControlError`, which names the failing operation and subclasses `httpx.TransportError` so every existing classification (readiness probing, `execution_failure()`) behaves exactly as before. Which of the five operations fails is still unknown; that is what the next run answers.

56. **`error_type` on a completion study names a failure inside episode execution.** It is written by `completion_study._execute`'s own `except Exception` around `execute_slots`, so it is not the runner's HTTP polling. I attributed Phase 13's 11 `ReadError`s to the runner before checking where the field is set.

57. **A reproducible mechanism is not necessarily THE mechanism.** Phase 14's tidy explanation - httpx's default `keepalive_expiry=5.0` equals uvicorn's default `timeout_keep_alive=5`, so the client writes into a socket the server just closed - is real, reproducible, and **not what happened**. httpcore prunes expired connections before selection (measured pool reuse 0 of 42 in the 5-6s band), `keepalive_expiry=60` against a 5s server gave 0 failures in 120 trials, and the reproducible window is ~2ms wide *below* 5.0s while 10 of the 11 real failures sit at >=5.054s. Always ask whether your reproduced window actually contains the field events. The investigator did not, and rated the hypothesis "high" on the same page where it recorded the refuting evidence.

58. **Localisation and causation are separate claims with separate evidence.** Phase 14 identified the failing call (`create()`) with total confidence from pure structure - which awaits sit outside a try, and which store writes are missing - and that conclusion survived every attack. The *cause* of the transport failure remains unexplained. Shipping the correct fix did not require knowing the cause, and pretending to know it would have put a refuted mechanism into the permanent record.

59. **Count your evidence strands before calling them independent.** Phase 13 reported three confirmations - no `episodes` record, no events, no `episode_registry` row. They are one: `events.episode_id` is a foreign key to `episode_registry`, and `episode_registry` is written only by `put_record` for `kind=='episodes'`, in the same transaction. The conclusion held; the stated strength did not.

60. **Idempotency is per-operation, and you must measure it rather than reason about it.** Measured on the real simulator: `create` with the same episode_id yields two distinct worlds; the **non-observer** `advance` goes tick 2 -> 4 and double-applies, which can flip whether a fault triggered and therefore whether a pair can qualify; the **observer** `advance` is latched by `horizon_done` and safely returns 409 on a repeat; `snapshot` and `faults` are byte-identical. I had asserted the observer advance was unsafe. It is not. A retry placed in the shared `control()` helper would have silently changed what the gate measures - which is why the anti-regression test asserting the other four operations are called exactly once matters more than the retry itself.

61. **"It failed zero times" needs the same scrutiny as "it fired zero times" (see item 50).** I recorded that the five protected teardown control calls never failed. They failed 3 times, and each one killed a completion study: the episode became `LAB_ERROR`, the evidence gateway rejected it for `lifecycle != 'COMPLETED'`, and the study died labelled `EvidenceDenied` instead of `ReadError`. A fix aimed only at the loud failure mode leaves the quiet one running.

62. **When two timers have the same value, the one that starts EARLIER wins, and that is the bug.** uvicorn and httpx both default to 5 seconds, which looks symmetric and is not: uvicorn arms its keep-alive timer in `on_response_complete`, when it finishes *writing* a response, while httpx starts its expiry when the client finishes *reading* it. The server's deadline therefore always falls first, leaving a window in which the client legitimately reuses a connection the server is about to close. uvicorn's handler then calls `transport.close()` without draining the socket (`h11_impl.py:372`), and on macOS closing a socket with unread bytes emits **RST**, so the request is discarded before the ASGI app runs. That is the whole of FaultLab's 37% loss channel. The repair is to make the client's expiry comfortably lower (2.0s against 5s), so the client always closes first.

63. **Ask whether the pattern you are trying to explain is actually there.** I spent a round demanding an explanation for "zero failures above 6 seconds" and briefed agents that any mechanism explaining only the lower edge was a story. There is no upper edge: the hazard decays from 5.0s with a ~0.4-0.5s scale, under which zero above 6s has P = 0.68, and the exposure above 6s is not even comparable because gap length is driven by the preceding episode's telemetry size. The p = 0.0009 I was handed for that edge became p = 0.02-0.21 under stratification. A sharp premise stated confidently will send four agents chasing it.

64. **Measured beats reasoned, twice over, in the same fix.** The first `create()` retry had two defects that no amount of reading would have surfaced: a fixed backoff ladder resonates with a periodic cause (12 exposures lost of 14 unjittered, 1 of 14 jittered), and `CREATE_BACKOFF` was indexed without a clamp while being one element shorter than `CREATE_ATTEMPTS`, so raising the attempt count raised `IndexError` instead of retrying. Also worth keeping: against a cause that simply persists, the ladder's **span** is the protection and the attempt count is nearly irrelevant - 3 attempts cover ~0.75s, 4 cover 1.5s, 5 cover 3.0s.

65. **A read-out can quietly stop measuring what it is named after.** Phase 15's declared primary - how often the gate accepts the policy - had become largely a measurement of the untriggered-fault rate, because Phase 11 removed the quality rejections that used to dominate it. A pass now essentially means "all 36 fault-bearing episodes fired". The nuisance rate had already moved 1.28% -> 2.75% between the two phases being compared, which alone swings the expected headline from 0.63 to 0.37 with the gate unchanged. The fix was to pre-declare the decomposition: headline, the nuisance rate with a Wilson interval quoted beside it every time, and the conditional figure. **That conditional turned out to be the best result the project has: of clean batches 9 of 10 are accepted, of batches with an untriggered fault 0 of 10.** Ask periodically whether a long-running metric still measures its own name.

66. **Declare the stronger figure before the run, or you cannot use it.** The quality-rejection contrast (8 of 13 vs 0 of 20, p = 0.000093) is ~400x stronger than the declared primary and would have been produced automatically. Quoting it afterwards would have been post-hoc read-out selection - the exact Phase 11 weakness the phase claimed to be fixing. It was declared as a named secondary with its comparator, which is what made it quotable when Phase 15 then broke the zero streak with 1 of 20.

67. **Check your own integrity claims before offering them as proof.** The first Phase 15 declaration asserted "algorithm_hash is unchanged" as evidence no acceptance logic had moved. It was false: `assess_pairs` lives in `completion_study.py`, which Phases 13-14 edited. The function's own text was not touched, but no byte-for-byte proof against Phase 13 exists, because the file is untracked and that phase took no source freeze. Record a function-level hash going forward rather than leaning on a tree hash that a neighbouring edit invalidates.

68. **Check that the recommended next step can succeed at all before costing it.** The Phase 15 handoff recommended "run full completion studies until a policy is ACCEPTED". No completion study can write ACCEPTED: the path never calls `commit_promotion`, the only promotion predicate needs the incumbent to fail a C1-C8 check, and the spec excludes completion-only sources. A goal that the code and the spec both forbid cannot be bought with more attempts. Read the acceptance code path end to end before estimating anything.

69. **A default is part of the experiment.** `actor_context_protocol or ACTOR_CONTEXT_PROTOCOL_V1` meant every full study ever run used the context whose citation corruption killed 4 of 5 step candidates, and the standard launcher cannot pass anything else. Phases 11-15 used v4 only because the replay runner passed it. Before a new mode of run, list every parameter that defaults silently and pin it in the runner, not in memory.

70. **A second draw at temperature 0 is not a second draw.** The Mechanic's slot 2 repeated slot 1 in 20 of 25 studies and added the step 0 of 22 times. Estimating per-study yield as 1-(1-p)^2 doubled the apparent chances. Count distinct outputs, not calls.

71. **A single-shot stage caps the whole pipeline.** "The gate certifies clean batches 9 of 10" is true, but a study cannot choose a clean batch: validation runs once and a working policy passes ~9 of 28. A cost model has to use the per-attempt rate at every single-shot stage.

72. **"0 of N when X" can be a definition, not a finding.** Phase 15's "0 of 10 accepted with an untriggered fault" is forced by `assess_pairs`. Before quoting a contrast, ask whether the rule already guarantees one side of it.

73. **A success criterion can be satisfied by the selection itself.** The unchanged gate's total gain is automatic once a candidate completes the source case, because the source case is also in the validation set and the baseline was chosen to fail it. "Improves a fresh case" needs its own condition. At a gain of one, a single baseline lapse can supply it (~11% for a source-only policy), so Phase 17 requires L-B0 >= 2 on one case.

74. **A fix can create the next failure mode; review the fixes too.** Round 1 made the runner refuse any campaign without a charge bound, which would also have refused every campaign whose study never started and ended the run for good after one failed start. Round 1 also added a SIGTERM handler that would have stopped the very study it should have left running. Round 2, run on the fixes alone, caught both. One review round is not enough when the round changes the code.

75. **A test that loads code at collection time can report green for a file that is now red.** The "622 passed" run had already imported the runner before its breaker default changed from 4 to 5, so the new test file on disk was failing. Re-run the files you just touched after the last edit, not before.

76. **A fake that is kinder than the real service hides the failure you most need to see.** The first runner fake always returned a number for `/budget`, so the ledger-less-campaign abort (item 74) passed its test. Another fake only became idle when a study's status was read, which hung a test. Model the fake on the real route's fallback bodies and state transitions, and cap every wait in tests.

77. **A step that is present is not a step that fires.** Two of Phase 17's four step-containing policies gated `retry_original_request` on `notification_operation_pending`. After a transient failure the status reads UNKNOWN, never PENDING, so the retry never ran (0/3 each). A predicate that checks the op's presence overstates working repairs. Check the condition the step is gated on against the statuses the fault actually produces.

78. **Side rules decide validation.** Attempt 4's policy repaired the target and the sibling case, then deferred on a still-PENDING upgrade with no wait, turning 3 `CORRECTLY_REJECTED` into 3 `SAFE_UNRESOLVED`. The certified policy waits a tick and re-reads before deciding. When a generated repair fails validation, look at its non-target rules first. And a draft sentence of mine said the certified policy's rules "never fire on a clean refusal"; they do fire. Describe a mechanism from the event log, not from reading the rule text.

79. **Check your account against the event log before you say it.** Right after Phase 18's verdict I told the user the failing run was "the only one of the 18 where the fix did anything". The policy acted in 10 of 18 candidate trials; the true, narrower fact was that the failure was the only notification-side action. Also: `usage.policy_steps` counts steps *recorded*, not *executed*; the failing episode shows 4, of which 1 ran. Count `policy_step` events with `executed: true`.

80. **A tally from memory is not a count.** Mid-investigation I told the user "over 2,500" re-sends preceded the drift; the query I had just run summed to 1,655. Add the numbers before quoting them, every time.

81. **When one failure decides a verdict, compare the lab's log with the world's before blaming the candidate.** Phase 18's only failure had a report that was true about the world. The lab dispatched 5 requests and the simulator recorded 4. An automatic same-`call_id` re-send after a timeout never reached the world, but the lab advanced its tick for it. Every later observation was stamped one tick past the world's decision tick, so C6 failed. Store-wide this drift occurs in 4 of 4,791 scored episodes, all C6 violations, **two of them baseline**. It is a measurement defect, not a policy behaviour. And a post-hoc explanation never earns a re-run on a spent partition: the declared verdict stays HARM.

82. **An open dashboard is part of the experiment.** A browser tab polling the lab backend once a second, through a Vite dev server left running since September 13, re-read and parsed the entire episode and evidence tables on the event loop. The lab was frozen about 0.8 s of every second while "idle", at 60% CPU. Any lab request that had to open a new connection during a freeze timed out without being sent, while the broker still counted its tick. That was Phase 18's only failure, and it failed two baseline episodes. Before a paid run, close the dashboard or confirm the loop is responsive (`artifacts/phase19-2026-09-23/diagnosis/loop_latency.py`), and run `scripts/experiments/check_clock_drift.py` afterwards.

83. **Refute your first mechanism by experiment, not by reading.** My first hypothesis blamed the *simulator*: its global lock freezes its loop, the client hangs up, and the POST is dropped before its body is read. A 30-second probe refuted it (12 of 12 recorded). The next probe found the real mechanism on the *client* side in minutes: 12 of 12 lost on a fresh connection, 0 of 24 otherwise.

Most of those re-sends followed a 503 on a connection that stayed open, where this failure cannot happen. The right base is requests that had to open a new connection: 0 of 372 lost before, 4 of 363 after, p ≈ 0.06. I also wrote that all four losses were re-sends; one was a first send.
